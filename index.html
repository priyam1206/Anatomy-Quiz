Overview
Pulp Structure
Soft connective tissue supporting the dentin. Progressing from the dentin-pulp junction inwards, it exhibits 4 distinct zones.
1. Odontoblast Zone
Location
Peripheral layer, closely related to the predentin.
Cell Structure
Basally located nucleus, supranuclear rough ER and golgi apparatus. Mitochondria and lysosomes throughout cytoplasm.
Function
Produce/secrete extracellular matrix of predentine (major protein: Type I Collagen) and bio-mineralization of dentine. Rate reduces with age.
Process
Extends from cell body up till the DEJ (Dentinoenamel Junction), contained in a dentinal tubule. Has multiple lateral processes.
2. Cell-Free Zone (Weil)
Location
Below the odontoblast layer; prominent in the coronal pulp.
Characteristics
Nearly devoid of cells, ~40 microns wide.
Traversed by
Blood vessels, unmyelinated nerves, and cytoplasmic processes of fibroblasts.
3. Cell-Rich Zone
Location
Beneath the cell-free zone; prominent in the coronal pulp.
Characteristics
Presence of major nerves/vessels. Discernible due to higher density of fibroblasts than the pulp proper.
Contents
Fibroblasts, undifferentiated mesenchymal cells, defense cells (macrophages, lymphocytes), blood capillaries, nerves.
4. Pulp Core
Location
The central connective tissue mass (pulp proper).
Contents
Fibroblasts (most abundant cell type), larger blood vessels, nerves, undifferentiated mesenchymal cells, collagen-fiber bundles.
Defense Cells
Macrophages are frequently located in the perivascular area (around vessels).
